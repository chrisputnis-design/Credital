The idea in one loan

Same $500. Very different value.

Purchasing power varies by country. $500 is roughly a week of rent in New York. In Johannesburg, for many households, it's a month's pay. Credital moves capital from where it's abundant to where it's scarce, and pays both sides for it.

The credit rating engine

One rating. One price. No black box.

Every borrower gets a bond-style rating from AAA to CCC before any loan is listed. The rating measures how likely they are to repay. Two things then set the price: the rating and the loan length. More risk or more time means a higher yield.

How a rating is built

  • Identity first. KYC and AML checks before anyone is rated.
  • Credit bureau where one exists. We don't ignore proven history.
  • Alternative data where it doesn't. Mobile-money history, bank transactions, utility and rent payments, verified income.

How the rate is set

Yield (APR) = Base rate for the rating × Term factor

The base rate is quoted at a 12-month term. The term factor adds 0.8% of the rate for every month beyond 12, and takes it off for every month under. Rates are always shown as annualised APR and APY, so you can compare them with a bond, a savings account or a fund.

For lenders

Consumer credit: the asset class banks kept for themselves.

Banks earn a spread by lending to households. Credital gives you access to that same yield, one small, rated loan at a time, alongside the ETFs and stocks you already hold.

A different engine of return

Stocks pay you through price moves. Bonds pay coupons. Consumer loans pay monthly instalments driven by households repaying, not by market sentiment.

  • Monthly cash flow of interest and principal
  • APR and APY on every loan, comparable with bonds and savings
  • Exposure to households and economies an index fund can't reach

Not risk-free: consumer credit can suffer in recessions. It just doesn't move in lockstep with your equities.

Trust & risk

Built for trust. Honest about risk.

A marketplace is only as good as the people on it and the way it handles things going wrong. Here's how Credital is designed to work.

KYC and AML on both sides

ID and document checks for every lender and borrower, sanctions and politically-exposed-person screening, and ongoing transaction monitoring.

Scoring that works without a bureau

Alternative data is used only with the borrower's consent, models are tested for bias, and ratings update as repayment history builds.

When a borrower misses a payment

Reminders first, then a restructuring offer, then collections through local partners. Money recovered is passed to lenders after collection costs. Recoveries are often partial.

Provision fund (planned)

A share of fees is set aside to absorb part of credit losses. It is not a guarantee: it can be used up, and it will not cover every loss.

Currency risk, made visible

Borrowers repay in the currency they earn. Lenders see the currency of every loan, can cap exposure per currency, and can choose hedged loans where a fair hedge exists. Currency moves can cut or lift your return.

Client money kept apart

Lender and borrower funds are planned to sit in segregated accounts with regulated partner banks, separate from Credital's own money.

Lending carries risk of loss.

You can lose some or all of the money you lend. Only lend what you can afford to lose.

  • •Default risk. Borrowers may not repay, and recoveries may be low.
  • •Currency risk. A weaker borrower currency can wipe out your yield.
  • •Liquidity risk. Your principal comes back as loans are repaid. Early exit isn't guaranteed.
  • •Platform and regulatory risk. Rules differ by country and can change.
  • •No safety net. Not a bank deposit, and not covered by any deposit guarantee scheme.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Fees and limits below are illustrative and will be confirmed at launch.

What happens if a borrower defaults, and can I lose money?

Yes, you can lose money. Some borrowers will not repay. After a missed payment we send reminders, offer to restructure, and then pursue collection through local partners. Recovered money goes to lenders after collection costs, and recoveries are often only partial.

A provision fund, financed from a share of fees, is planned to absorb part of the losses. It isn't a guarantee. Your main protection is spreading money across many loans, and only lending what you can afford to lose.

How does cross-border lending work with currencies?

Borrowers borrow and repay in the currency they earn, so they aren't exposed to swings against a currency they don't use. That means lenders carry the exchange-rate risk on cross-border loans.

We show the currency of every loan, let you set per-currency limits in auto-invest, and offer hedged loans where a fair hedge is available. If the borrower's currency falls 10% against yours, that can wipe out a year's yield or more. Try it in the calculator above.

What does Credital cost?

Joining is free. In our current model, lenders pay a servicing fee of 1.0% a year on money out on loan, and borrowers pay a one-off origination fee of 2%, taken from the payout. Any currency conversion is charged at a disclosed margin.

Every fee is shown before you commit. There are no hidden charges. These figures are illustrative and final pricing will be published at launch.

What's the minimum I can invest?

We're planning a $100 minimum to start and $20 minimum per loan, so even a small balance can be spread across many borrowers. Limits may differ by country.

How do you score people who have no credit bureau file?

With their consent, we look at how they actually manage money: mobile-money history, bank transactions, utility and rent payments, and verified income. Where a bureau exists, we use it too.

Those signals feed a statistical model that produces a rating from AAA to CCC. It is monitored for bias, reviewed by people, and updated as real repayments come in.

How is the interest rate decided?

By two inputs only: the borrower's rating and the loan duration. Higher risk and longer duration mean a higher rate. It's fixed for the life of the loan and shown as an annualised APR (and APY for lenders), so you can compare it with other assets or other loans.

Can I take my money out whenever I want?

Repayments arrive in your wallet each month and you can withdraw them. But money still lent out is tied up until borrowers repay, and early exit is not guaranteed. A secondary market is something we're exploring, not a promise.

Borrowers: what do I need, and how fast is it?

A phone, a government ID and a way to show how you handle money, such as mobile-money or bank history, or proof of income and rent. Verification takes minutes. Our target is to have the loan funded within 48 hours of approval. You see your rating, rate and every fee before you accept.
